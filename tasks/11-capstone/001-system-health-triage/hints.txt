Issue 1 (process): this is the same pattern as the fundamentals process-management task — pgrep -f to find it, kill or pkill -f to stop it.
Issue 2 (permissions): chmod 640 sets the right mode; chgrp (or chown :group) fixes group ownership — use 'id -gn' to see your own primary group name.
Issue 3 (disk): find incident/data -type f -printf '%s %p\n' | sort -rn | head will show you which file is way bigger than the rest — then just rm that one file.
Full solution:
  pkill -f lab_capstone_leak
  chmod 640 incident/app-output.log
  chgrp "$(id -gn)" incident/app-output.log
  rm "$(find incident/data -type f -printf '%s %p\n' | sort -rn | head -n1 | awk '{print $2}')"
